## Discussed topics

### English conditional sentences

The teacher guided the student through various conditional sentence structures, explaining the patterns and relationships between different parts of conditional sentences.

- Details
    
    - Be: Explained the zero conditional (general truths): "If you heat water to 100 degrees Celsius, it boils"
    - Be: Taught first conditional (possible future): "If I have enough money next year, I will buy a new computer"
    - Be: Demonstrated second conditional (hypothetical present/future): "If I had a million dollars right now, I would quit my job"
    - Be: Explained third conditional (impossible past): "If she had woken up earlier, she would have caught the plane"
    - LUIZ HENRIQUE: Struggled with distinguishing between different conditional forms but made progress during the lesson
- Conclusion
    
    - The pattern for zero conditional: If + present simple, present simple
    - The pattern for first conditional: If + present simple, will + base verb
    - The pattern for second conditional: If + past simple, would + base verb
    - The pattern for third conditional: If + past perfect, would have + past participle

### Modal verbs

The teacher introduced and practiced various modal verbs to express possibility, obligation, and probability.

- Details
    
    - Be: Asked the student to translate sentences using modals like "might," "must," "can," and "could"
    - LUIZ HENRIQUE: Practiced using "might" to express possibility: "She might not be in her 40s"
    - Be: Explained the difference between "must" (strong probability) and "might" (possibility)
    - LUIZ HENRIQUE: Learned to use "can't be" for impossibility: "It can't be summer"
- Conclusion
    
    - "Must" is used for strong probability or obligation
    - "Might/could" are used for possibility
    - "Can't" is used for impossibility
    - "Should" is used for advice or recommendation

### Describing appearances and making comparisons

The teacher had the student practice describing people and making comparisons.

- Details
    
    - Be: Asked the student to translate phrases about appearance: "cabelo escuro e cachado" (dark, curly hair)
    - LUIZ HENRIQUE: Practiced age descriptions: "in his 20s," "in her 40s"
    - Be: Explained the difference between "looks like" (comparison) and "looks" + adjective (description)
- Conclusion
    
    - "Looks like" is used for comparing someone to another person
    - "Looks" + adjective is used for describing characteristics
