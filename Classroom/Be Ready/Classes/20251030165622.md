### English language practice: Future tense with "will" and "going to"

Be taught Leticia about the differences between using "will" and "going to" for future tense.

- Details
    
    - Be: Asked Leticia what she knew about the difference between "will" and "going to"
    - Leticia: Wasn't sure about the grammatical explanation but could use them in phrases
    - Be: Provided examples and practice sentences
    - Be: Explained that "will" is often used with opinions and predictions based on personal views
    - Be: Demonstrated that "going to" is used for planned events
    - Leticia: Practiced with various sentences about future events
- Conclusion
    
    - When expressing opinions about the future, "will" is often more appropriate
    - For planned events or certainties, "going to" is typically used
    - Both forms can sometimes be correct but with slightly different nuances

Be helped Leticia with several preposition and collocation issues in English.

- Details
    
    - Be: Corrected Leticia's use of "near from" to simply "near"
    - Be: Explained that "close to" requires the preposition while "near" doesn't
    - Be: Practiced various phrases like "look at," "look in," "look into"
    - Be: Corrected "do a table" to "make a table" and "do a product" to "make a product"
    - Be: Clarified the difference between "remember" and "remind"
    - Be: Pointed out the false cognate "pretend" (which Leticia used instead of "intend")
- Conclusion
    
    - English prepositions don't always follow logical patterns and often need to be memorized
    - Collocations (word partnerships) are important for natural-sounding English
