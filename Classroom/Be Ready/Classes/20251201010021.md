## Key Takeaways and Summary of Learning Objectives

- English verb patterns often follow arbitrary rules that must be memorized rather than logically deduced
- Certain verbs like "offer," "refuse," "admit," and "regret" follow specific patterns with infinitives or gerunds
- Past perfect continuous tense (had been + verb-ing) is used for actions that were ongoing before another past action
- Present perfect continuous (have been + verb-ing) is used for actions continuing from the past to present
- Verbs can change meaning depending on whether they're followed by infinitives or gerunds (e.g., "deny doing" vs. "deny to do")

## Topic 1: Verb Patterns with Infinitives and Gerunds

The class began with exercises on verb patterns, focusing on which verbs take infinitives (to + verb) and which take gerunds (verb + ing). The teacher emphasized that these patterns are often arbitrary and must be memorized. They reviewed examples like "offer to take," "refuse to go," "admit stealing," "regret saying," and "threaten to tell." The teacher highlighted that some verbs like "insist" take specific prepositions (insist on) followed by gerunds. They practiced translating phrases from Portuguese to English to reinforce these patterns, with the teacher explaining that while Portuguese might use different structures, English follows specific rules for each verb.

## Topic 2: Past Perfect and Present Perfect Continuous

The lesson covered the distinction between past perfect continuous (had been + verb-ing) and present perfect continuous (have been + verb-ing). The teacher explained that past perfect continuous is used for actions that were ongoing before another past action occurred, while present perfect continuous is used for actions continuing from the past to the present. They analyzed examples like "It had been raining for 2 hours when we finally went home" and "I have been working for 2 hours," emphasizing how the tense changes based on the timeframe and whether another past action is mentioned.

## Topic 3: Verbs with Different Meanings Based on Pattern

The class explored how some verbs change meaning depending on whether they're followed by infinitives or gerunds. For example, "deny doing something" means to claim you didn't do something that happened in the past, while "deny to do something" means to refuse an offer. Similarly, they discussed how "remind someone to do" differs from "remind someone of something." The teacher emphasized that these distinctions are important for accurate communication and provided multiple examples for practice.

### Relevant Q&A

Be: Perfeito, tá? Então você também pode sugerir ir como fala, sugerir ir.
