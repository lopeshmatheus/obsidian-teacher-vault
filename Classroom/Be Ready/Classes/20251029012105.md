- Conclusion

    - Memory skills developed more with increased responsibility in adulthood

### Driving experience and navigation

Discussion about when Luiz started driving and how navigation methods have changed over time.

- Details
    
    - Luiz: Got his driver's license on his 18th birthday in 2009 as a gift from his parents
    - Luiz: At his first job, his boss printed maps for employees to find client locations
    - Luiz: Recently got a motorcycle license but hasn't purchased a motorcycle yet
    - Luiz: Plans to buy a motorcycle for trips to mountains and beaches, not for daily commuting
    - Be: Shared that when he got his license in 2019, GPS was already common, making it unnecessary to memorize routes
- Conclusion
    
    - Navigation methods have evolved significantly from printed maps to GPS
    - People who learned to drive before GPS was common may have better memory for directions
    - Luiz intends to buy a motorcycle for recreational purposes

### English grammar: Present perfect tense

The teacher focused on teaching Luiz about the present perfect tense and when to use it compared to simple present and past tenses.

- Details
    
    - Be: Explained that "I haven't had a memorable day in years" is correct rather than "I don't have a memorable day in years"
    - Be: Clarified that present perfect is not more formal than other tenses, but serves different grammatical functions
    - Be: Demonstrated how time expressions like "in years" or "for years" require present perfect in English
    - Be: Showed how Portuguese uses present tense in contexts where English requires present perfect
    - Luiz: Asked if the choice between tenses depends on formality or context
- Conclusion
    
    - Time expressions like "in years" require present perfect tense in English
    - The present perfect is not more difficult or formal than other tenses, just different
    - Translation between languages doesn't always work directly due to different grammatical structures

### Present perfect continuous

The teacher briefly introduced the present perfect continuous tense.

- Details
    
    - Be: Explained that "I study English" can be expressed as "I have been studying English" in certain contexts
    - Be: Demonstrated how "I have been having my car for 10 years" is the correct form for possession over time
    - Be: Showed how present perfect continuous relates to simple present in expressing ongoing actions
- Conclusion
    
    - Present perfect continuous is another alternative for expressing present actions with certain time expressions
    - This tense will be covered more thoroughly in future lessons
