## Discussed topics

### English vocabulary related to weddings and relationships

The teacher introduced various terms related to weddings and relationships, asking Caleb to translate or identify the correct terms.

- Details
    
    - Be: Asked how to say "ficar noivos" in English
    - Caleb: Initially suggested "wedding" but then corrected to "get engaged"
    - Be: Explained the difference between "fiancé" (male) and "fiancée" (female) though pronounced the same
    - Be: Clarified that "bride" refers to a woman on her wedding day, while "fiancée" is used during the engagement period
    - Caleb: Noted he had seen "bride" used before weddings on shirts at pre-wedding parties
    - Be: Explained that "groom" is the male equivalent of "bride" on the wedding day
    - Be: Taught that "best man" is the term for the groom's witness/supporter and "bridesmaid" for the bride's
- Conclusion
    
    - Various wedding-related terms were reviewed including: get engaged, fiancé/fiancée, bride/groom, best man/bridesmaid
    - Different terms apply at different stages of the relationship process

### Present perfect tense usage

The teacher focused on teaching when to use present perfect tense (have/has + past participle) versus simple past.

- Details
    
    - Be: Explained that "never" with present perfect is used for lifetime experiences
    - Be: Demonstrated that "I have never gotten married" is correct for lifetime experiences
    - Be: Showed that "She has gotten married three times" uses the same structure
    - Caleb: Practiced forming sentences with present perfect
    - Be: Explained that "for X years" often requires present perfect tense
- Conclusion
    
    - Present perfect is used for experiences over a lifetime or continuing situations
    - Words like "never," "ever," "already," "yet," and time expressions like "for X years" often trigger present perfect

### Past perfect tense practice

The teacher introduced past perfect tense and compared it with present perfect.

- Details
    
    - Be: Explained that past perfect (had + past participle) is used for actions completed before another past action
    - Caleb: Practiced forming sentences like "I had played" and "She had gone"
    - Be: Noted that past perfect might be easier for Portuguese speakers than present perfect because there's a clearer translation
    - Be: Used examples from the story about George and Mabel: "He had not seen Mabel for 7 years"
- Conclusion
    
    - Past perfect is used to describe actions completed before another point in the past
    - The structure follows "had + past participle" pattern
