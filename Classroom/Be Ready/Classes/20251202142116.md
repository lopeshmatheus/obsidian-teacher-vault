## Overview

This class focused on English verb patterns and prepositions, particularly how verbs combine with infinitives, gerunds, and prepositions. The instructor (Be) guided the student (Caleb) through various verb constructions, emphasizing the differences between Portuguese and English grammar patterns. The lesson covered how to properly form phrases with verbs like "offer," "refuse," "admit," "persuade," and "insist," highlighting the correct prepositions and verb forms that follow them.

### Key Concepts or Theories:

- Verb + to + infinitive patterns (e.g., "offer to help," "refuse to go")
- Verb + preposition + gerund patterns (e.g., "insist on paying," "admit stealing")
- Negative infinitive constructions (e.g., "promise not to tell" vs. "don't promise to tell")
- Exceptions to standard patterns (e.g., "look forward to seeing")
- Translation of Portuguese verb patterns into correct English equivalents

### Important Questions Raised:

## Key Takeaways and Summary of Learning Objectives

- Certain verbs in English are consistently followed by specific prepositions and verb forms (infinitive or gerund)
- The pattern "verb + to + infinitive" applies to verbs like offer, refuse, agree, promise, persuade, and invite
- The pattern "verb + preposition + gerund" applies to verbs like insist on, accuse of, apologize for
- When forming negatives with infinitives, "not" comes before the infinitive (e.g., "I promised not to tell")
- Some verb patterns in Portuguese translate differently in English, requiring specific prepositions
- The structure of sentences changes based on whether the verb is followed by an infinitive or gerund
- Exceptions exist where "to" is followed by a gerund instead of an infinitive (e.g., "look forward to seeing")

## Topic 1: Verb Patterns with Infinitives

The class began with exploring verbs that are followed by the infinitive form (to + base verb). The instructor emphasized that verbs like "offer," "refuse," "agree," "promise," "persuade," and "invite" are followed by "to" plus the base form of the verb. For example, "offer to help," "refuse to go," "agree to do," "promise to call," and "invite someone to a party." The instructor guided Caleb through forming sentences like "Jack offered to take me to the airport" and "I promised not to tell anyone." The lesson highlighted that when forming negative infinitives, "not" comes before the infinitive (e.g., "I persuaded my sister not to go out" rather than "to not go out").

## Topic 2: Verb Patterns with Gerunds

The second major topic covered verbs that are followed by prepositions and then gerunds (-ing form). The instructor explained that verbs like "insist," "accuse," "apologize," "admit," and "regret" follow specific patterns. For example, "insist on doing," "accuse of doing," "apologize for being," "admit doing," and "regret doing." Caleb practiced forming sentences like "He admitted stealing some money," "She accused Carl of stealing a car," and "I apologized for being late." The instructor emphasized that after prepositions, the gerund form (-ing) is always used rather than the infinitive.

## Topic 3: Exceptions and Special Cases

The final portion of the class addressed exceptions to the standard verb patterns and special cases. The instructor explained that while most verbs follow predictable patterns, some expressions like "look forward to" require a gerund even though they contain "to" (which typically precedes an infinitive). The instructor also discussed how negative forms work with different verb structures, emphasizing that the placement of "not" changes depending on whether the verb is followed by an infinitive or a gerund. Additionally, the class covered how certain Portuguese expressions translate differently in English, such as "pagar pela refeição" becoming "pay for the meal."
