### English grammar: Relative clauses

Be introduced relative clauses and explained when to use "who," "which," and "that."

- Details
    
    - Be: Explained that "who" is used for people, "which" for things, and "that" can be used for both
    - Caleb: Practiced using relative clauses in sentences like "a man who didn't want to marry that woman"
    - Be: Provided examples to demonstrate the difference between the relative pronouns
- Conclusion
    
    - Using "who" for people and "which" for things shows more mastery of English
    - "That" is more generic and can be used for both people and things

### Pronunciation practice: Short and long vowel sounds

The class worked on distinguishing between short and long vowel sounds in English.

- Details
    
    - Be: Explained the difference between words like "ship/sheep" and "list/least"
    - Caleb: Practiced pronouncing these minimal pairs
    - Be: Demonstrated how "i" produces a short sound while "ea" produces a long sound
- Conclusion
    
    - Short "i" sounds appear in words like "ship," "list," and "tip"
    - Long "ee" sounds appear in words like "sheep," "least," and "sheet"
    - These distinctions are important to avoid miscommunication

### Vocabulary: "Escape from" and "marry/get married"

The class discussed specific vocabulary related to the story.

- Details
    
    - Be: Explained that "escape" should be followed by "from"
    - Caleb: Practiced saying "she escaped from her husband"
    - Be: Clarified the difference between "marry someone" and "get married"
    - Caleb: Learned about the ambiguity in English when saying someone "married" another person
- Conclusion
    
    - "Escape" should be followed by "from" when indicating what someone is escaping from
    - "Marry someone" is used when specifying the person being married
    - "Get married" is used when talking about the act of marriage without specifying a partner
