The class began with a review of sensory verbs and their correct usage in English. The instructor explained that different verbs are used depending on which sense is being employed: "looks like" for visual comparisons, "sounds like" for auditory comparisons, "tastes like" for flavor comparisons, and "feels like" for tactile comparisons. ## Topic 3: Complex Sentence Structures

The class covered complex sentence structures, particularly those involving beliefs and expectations. The instructor explained how to form sentences with "is thought to," "is believed to," and "is expected to" as alternatives to Portuguese expressions like "acredita-se que." The student learned that these structures follow a pattern: subject + is thought/believed/expected + to + verb. For example, "The robber is thought to be a young man," "The economy is expected to grow," and "This mushroom is believed to be poisonous." The instructor emphasized that direct translation between languages doesn't always work, and finding alternative structures that convey the same meaning is often necessary.
