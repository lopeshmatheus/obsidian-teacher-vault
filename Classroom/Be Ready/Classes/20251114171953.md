## Topic 2: Past Tense Forms

The instructor explained the differences between simple past (I watched), present perfect (I have watched), and past perfect (I had watched) tenses. They emphasized that the choice between simple past and present perfect often depends on time expressions rather than simply whether time is mentioned. For example, "I watched this film yesterday" (simple past with specific time) versus "I have watched this film three times" (present perfect with result continuing to present). The instructor also introduced the past perfect tense for actions completed before another past action. They practiced sentences with time markers like "just," "already," and "yet," which typically require present perfect tense.

### Relevant Q&A

Leticia: My sister just started a new job.

Be: Or...

Leticia: My sister just has started in a new job.

Be: It's not that you cannot say that, especially if it's more poetic, if it's more formal, it's okay, but usually we invert this. We don't say just have, we say have just.

Leticia: I have never heard this word before.

Be: Perfect. Now you have.
