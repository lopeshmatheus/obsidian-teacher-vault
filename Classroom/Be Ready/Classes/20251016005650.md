The teacher explained the difference between these two expressions in English and their Portuguese equivalents.

- Details

### Present perfect vs. simple past usage

A significant portion of the class was dedicated to explaining when to use present perfect versus simple past tense.

- Details
    
    - Be: Explained that time expressions often determine which tense to use
    - Be: Demonstrated that "recently" typically requires present perfect
    - Be: Showed that "when" questions typically require simple past
    - anthony.silva: Practiced with examples like "I have traveled recently" vs. "When did you travel?"
- Conclusion
    
    - Time expressions like "recently" and "for [time period]" typically require present perfect
    - Question words like "when" typically require simple past
    - Native speakers sometimes break these rules, but it's important to know the standard usage

### Present perfect continuous usage

The teacher explained when to use present perfect continuous and its Portuguese equivalents.

- Details
    
    - Be: Explained that "have been + -ing" is used for ongoing actions that started in the past
    - Be: Clarified that "I have been working" translates to "eu tenho trabalhado" in Portuguese
    - anthony.silva: Practiced with examples like "I have been studying English for 10 years"
- Conclusion
    
    - Present perfect continuous is used for ongoing actions that started in the past and continue to the present
    - With time expressions like "for 10 years," present perfect or present perfect continuous must be used if the action is still ongoing
