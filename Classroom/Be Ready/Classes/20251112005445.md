## Discussed topics

### Story by William Somerset Maugham

The class began with reading a story about a man who abandoned his fiancée after 7 years apart.

- Details
    
    - Be: Introduced the story by William Somerset Maugham
    - Luana: Initially confused about who Mabel was in the story
    - Be: Clarified that Mabel was the female character, not the author
    - Luana: Summarized that George and Mabel had been apart for 7 years and George decided he couldn't marry her
    - Be: Explained that George's dilemma was choosing between telling Mabel the truth or running away
- Conclusion
    
    - George chose to run away and lie to Mabel in a letter rather than face her
    - The story illustrated the concept of a "dilemma" - having to choose between two bad options

### Past perfect tense (past of the past)

The teacher explained how to use past perfect tense to describe events that happened before another past event.

- Details
    
    - Be: Explained that when two past events compete, one needs to be put "further in the past"
    - Be: Demonstrated the structure with examples like "I had liked" and "I had been planning"
    - Luana: Practiced forming sentences like "She had been planning the wedding when he ran away"
    - Be: Provided additional examples with "I had been traveling with my family for 2 years when the money ran out"
- Conclusion
    
    - Past perfect (had + past participle) is used to show an action that happened before another past action
    - For continuous actions, "had been + -ing" form is used

### Relationship and wedding vocabulary

The teacher reviewed vocabulary related to relationships, engagements, and weddings.

- Details
    
    - Be: Asked Luana how to say various relationship terms in English
    - Luana: Correctly identified terms like "get engaged," "getting married," "make arrangements"
    - Be: Taught prepositions used with relationship verbs (engaged to, married to, divorced from)
    - Be: Introduced wedding-related vocabulary like "best man" and "bridesmaid"
- Conclusion
    
    - Relationship verbs often require specific prepositions in English
    - It's easier to memorize phrases like "divorced from" and then remove the preposition when not needed
