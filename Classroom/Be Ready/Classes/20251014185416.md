### English grammar lesson: "It depends" expressions

Teacher explained the correct usage of "it depends" and similar expressions

- Details
    
    - Be: Taught that "it depends" is the correct form, not "depends it"
    - Be: Explained other similar expressions like "it happens," "it rains"
    - Be: Demonstrated that "it depends on" requires the preposition "on"
    - anthony.silva: Practiced using these expressions in different contexts
- Conclusion
    
    - The correct form is "it depends on [something]"
    - Similar expressions follow the same pattern: "it happens," "it rains"

### English grammar lesson: Future tense

Discussion about different ways to express future actions in English

- Details
    
    - Be: Explained the difference between "will" and "going to" for future expressions
    - Be: Taught that predictions from present information use "will" while predictions based on past information use "going to"
    - anthony.silva: Practiced using both forms in different contexts
    - Be: Noted that in Portuguese there's only one form ("vai") but English has this nuance
- Conclusion
    
    - "Will" is used for predictions based on present information
    - "Going to" (or "gonna") is used for predictions based on past information

### English grammar lesson: "Don't mind" vs "Don't care"

Explanation of different expressions for showing indifference

- Details
    
    - Be: Explained the difference between "don't care" and "don't mind"
    - Be: Taught that "don't care about" is used when you have no consideration for something
    - Be: Demonstrated that "don't mind" is used when something doesn't bother you
    - anthony.silva: Practiced using "I don't mind working a lot"
- Conclusion
    
    - "Don't care about" means having no consideration for something
    - "Don't mind" means not being bothered by something

### English grammar lesson: Present perfect continuous

Brief discussion about using present perfect continuous tense

- Details
    
    - Be: Taught the structure "I've always been" for states that started in the past and continue to the present
    - anthony.silva: Practiced with examples like "I've always been a professor"
    - Be: Explained "I've been working a lot" as another example of this structure
- Conclusion
    
    - Present perfect continuous is used for actions that started in the past and continue to the present

### English grammar lesson: Causative structures

Introduction to causative structures with "have"

- Details
    
    - Be: Introduced the structure "have something done" for when others perform actions for you
    - Be: Provided examples: "I have my hair cut," "I had my house painted," "I had my car fixed"
    - anthony.silva: Successfully practiced using these structures
    - Be: Explained that this structure is used when other people do the action for you
- Conclusion
    
    - The structure "have + object + past participle" is used when others perform actions for you
    - Past tense becomes "had + object + past participle"
