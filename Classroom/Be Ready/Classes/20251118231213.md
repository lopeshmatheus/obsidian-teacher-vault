## Topic 1: Crime Vocabulary and Distinctions

The lesson introduced essential crime vocabulary, highlighting important distinctions that don't exist in Portuguese. A key difference was explained between "steal" and "rob" - in English, people steal objects but rob people or places. For example, "The criminal stole my bicycle" versus "He robbed my father." The instructor emphasized that this distinction doesn't exist in Portuguese, where "roubar" is used for both actions. Other vocabulary covered included "thief" (someone who steals), "robber" (someone who robs), "murderer" (someone who commits murder), and expressions like "break into" for home invasion.

## Topic 2: Passive Voice with Crime Vocabulary

The class covered extensive use of passive voice with crime vocabulary, focusing on the pattern of "be + past participle." The instructor explained that after forms of "be" (am, is, are, was, were), the past participle form of the verb must be used. Examples included "be arrested," "be caught," "be questioned," "be charged with," and "be found guilty." The lesson emphasized that this structure is similar to what follows "have" in perfect tenses. For instance, "She was arrested because she committed a crime" and "The murderer has never been caught."

### Relevant Q&A

LUIZ HENRIQUE: Be arrested.

Be: How can I say "ser pego"?

LUIZ HENRIQUE: Be catch?

Be: No. Be caught, perfect. Be catch is never going to be an option, because if you say, be catch, it's like saying be play.

## Topic 3: Special Expressions and Structures

The lesson covered several special expressions related to crime and legal processes. These included "be aware of" (estar ciente de), "keep out of sight" (manter fora de vista), "be accused of" (ser acusado de), "be charged with" (ser acusado no sistema legal), "be found guilty" (ser considerado culpado), and "be sentenced to prison" (ser sentenciado à prisão). The instructor also introduced the "have something done" structure, which can be used to express "ter algo roubado" (have something stolen) or to indicate that someone arranged for something to be done, as in "I had my car fixed."
