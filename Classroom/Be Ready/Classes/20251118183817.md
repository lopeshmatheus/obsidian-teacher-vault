## Topic 1: Crime Vocabulary and Distinctions

The lesson covered important distinctions between similar crime-related terms. A "thief" typically steals in public places or on the street, while a "burglar" specifically breaks into houses or premises to steal. The verb "steal" (steal/stole/stolen) is used when taking something portable, while "rob" is used for places, banks, or people. The instructor emphasized that "break into" is a phrasal verb that must be kept together when describing illegal entry into a house or building. Additional vocabulary included "killer" (someone who kills) and "murderer" (someone who commits murder), as well as legal terms like "arrested," "charged with," "questioned," "found guilty," and "sentenced to."

## Topic 2: Verb Forms and Tenses

The instructor explained the different forms of irregular verbs used to describe criminal activities. For example, "steal" (present), "stole" (past), and "stolen" (past participle). The lesson emphasized when to use each form, particularly distinguishing between simple past ("I stole") and present perfect ("I have stolen"). The instructor also covered passive voice constructions using "be" + past participle, such as "she was arrested," "he was caught," and "they were charged with." The student practiced forming sentences in different tenses and voices to describe various crime scenarios.
