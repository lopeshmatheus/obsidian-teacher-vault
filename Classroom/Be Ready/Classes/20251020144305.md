## Key takeaways

- English class focused on café vocabulary, ordering food and drinks, and practicing conversational skills
- Students completed homework review with 85% accuracy as a group
- Key grammar points covered: "I like it" vs "I like both/neither", countable vs uncountable nouns, and use of "from...to" for time ranges
- Students practiced role-playing café ordering scenarios to improve practical communication skills
- Vocabulary introduced included café menu items, tastes (bitter, sour, sweet), and ordering expressions

## Discussed topics

### Homework Review and Corrections

The class began with a comprehensive review of homework exercises focusing on food vocabulary and grammar structures.

- Details
    
    - Larissa: Struggled with "I like it" usage, particularly in questions about cereal preferences
    - Be: Explained that "I like it" is used when referring to something specific, while "I like it so much" adds emphasis without changing the core structure
    - Rafael: Completed exercises on breakfast preferences and food choices
    - Larissa Castilho: Worked through questions about cooking and meal preparation
    - Be: Clarified the difference between "I like both" (liking two things) and "I like neither" (liking none)
- Conclusion
    
    - Group achieved 85% accuracy on homework
    - Students need more practice distinguishing when to use "it" in "like" constructions
    - Double negatives are grammatically incorrect in formal English but may appear in some dialects

### Café Vocabulary and Menu Items

Students learned vocabulary related to cafés, including drinks, food items, and ordering expressions.

- Details
    
    - Be: Introduced vocabulary including "go to a café", "a cup of tea/coffee", "a slice of cake/pie"
    - Be: Taught ordering expressions: "Can I get", "I would like", "How much is", "That'll be"
    - Students: Learned the difference between "café" (coffee) and "coffee" (the drink), noting "cough" sounds like "tosse" (cough in Portuguese)
    - Be: Explained "here you go" as a common phrase when serving items
    - Be: Introduced taste vocabulary: bitter (coffee), sour (strawberries), sweet
- Conclusion
    
    - Students can now identify and pronounce common café menu items
    - Key expressions for ordering were practiced and understood
    - Pronunciation of "coffee" vs "café" was clarified to avoid confusion

### Personal Preferences Discussion

Students discussed their personal preferences regarding coffee, cafés, and food choices.

- Details
    
    - Larissa: Likes going to cafés, drinks black coffee with no sugar since 2019
    - Rafael: Prefers black coffee with no sugar since 2020, finds cafés expensive, orders "pingado" (coffee with a little milk) at work
    - Larissa Castilho: Does not like coffee or tea, prefers strawberry juice with sugar
    - Be: Shared opinion that undercooked "bolinho de chuva" (with creamy center) is best, and carrot cake is top-tier
    - Larissa: Believes orange cake is the best cake
    - Rafael: Prefers chocolate cake and cappuccino with cinnamon
- Conclusion
    
    - Students successfully expressed personal preferences in English
    - Vocabulary for describing food preferences was reinforced through natural conversation
    - Cultural differences in café culture and food preferences were discussed

### Cozy Corner Café Menu Exercise

Students analyzed a fictional café menu and answered comprehension questions about prices and options.

- Details
    
    - Be: Presented menu with opening hours (8 AM to 9 PM, Monday to Saturday)
    - Students: Practiced reading prices using "euros/dollars" format (e.g., "two euros ten")
    - Larissa: Identified orange juice as the only cold drink
    - Rafael: Listed fruit options: orange juice, apple pie, lemon tart
    - Larissa: Identified chicken sandwich as the only sandwich with meat
    - Be: Explained "white coffee" as coffee with milk (equivalent to "pingado")
- Conclusion
    
    - Students can read and understand café menus in English
    - Price format and pronunciation were clarified
    - Students identified all menu categories: drinks, cakes/desserts, sandwiches, ice cream

### Grammar Point: "From...To" Construction

Be taught students how to express time ranges and locations using "from...to".

- Details
    
    - Be: Explained opening hours format: "from 8 AM to 9 PM"
    - Larissa: Practiced saying "from Monday to Saturday" and "from Monday to Friday"
    - Larissa Castilho: Applied the structure to locations: "from Minas to São Paulo"
    - Rafael: Successfully constructed "from 8 AM to 9 PM"
- Conclusion
    
    - Students can now express time ranges and distance using "from...to"
    - Structure applies to both time and location contexts
    - Students demonstrated understanding through multiple examples

### Role-Playing Café Ordering Scenarios

Students practiced real-world café ordering through role-play exercises.

- Details
    
    - Be: First acted as waiter while students were customers
    - Rafael: Ordered black coffee and chocolate cake, asked about prices
    - Larissa: Ordered chocolate muffin and coffee, asked "how much is tea?"
    - Larissa and Rafael: Switched roles with Larissa as waiter and Rafael as customer
    - Rafael: Ordered black coffee, apple pie, and vanilla ice cream with caramel sauce
    - Be: Introduced "that'll be" for stating total prices and "can I get you" for offering items
- Conclusion
    
    - Students successfully practiced ordering in realistic scenarios
    - Communication strategies were emphasized over perfect grammar
    - Students gained confidence in handling café interactions in English

The class engaged in lively discussions about food preferences, particularly regarding cakes and ice cream.

    - Be: Explained "let's put a pin on that" as a business expression meaning to return to a topic later

- Conclusion

    - Common English expressions were introduced in context
    - Students learned to recognize and use idiomatic phrases
