## Topic 1: Crime-Related Vocabulary

The lesson covered important distinctions between related crime terms in English. "Theft" refers to the act of stealing something, while "robbery" involves taking something directly from a person, often with force or threats. "Burglary" specifically refers to breaking into a building to steal items. The person who commits these crimes is called a "thief," "robber," or "burglar" respectively. The lesson also introduced passive constructions commonly used when discussing being victimized by crime, such as "to be burglared" (when someone breaks into your home and steals your possessions) and "to be robbed" (when someone takes something from you directly). Additional vocabulary included "valuables" (valuable objects), expressions like "out of sight" (not visible), and "break into" (to enter a place by force).

## Topic 2: Street Safety Advice

The class analyzed eight important pieces of advice from a police crime prevention website about staying safe on the streets. These included: (1) Be prepared - plan your route, carry a charged phone and cash, tell someone where you're going; (2) Be assertive - walk with confidence to appear less vulnerable; (3) Be aware - avoid distractions like phones or loud music that reduce awareness; (4) Hide it - keep valuables out of sight; (5) Go against the flow - walk facing oncoming traffic to prevent thieves on bikes from approaching from behind; (6) Trust your instincts - avoid isolated areas at night and stick to well-lit, busy places; (7) Make a plan - discuss with friends what to do if separated; and (8) Look out for trouble - be careful with alcohol consumption and never leave drinks unattended. The instructor emphasized that appearing confident and aware makes one less likely to be targeted, as criminals typically seek easy targets.

## Topic 3: Home Security and Burglar Psychology

The lesson explored factors that influence burglars when choosing homes to target and strategies for home protection. They discussed how burglars tend to select houses with visual cover (trees and bushes) and where no one is home. The instructor explained that security measures like locks, cameras, and alarms don't necessarily make a home impossible to break into, but rather make it appear to be a more difficult target compared to other houses. This psychological deterrent often causes burglars to choose easier targets. The class also discussed common items stolen during burglaries (beyond money), with laptops and tablets being primary targets due to their value and prevalence in most homes. The instructor mentioned that burglars typically spend only 10-20 minutes searching a house.
