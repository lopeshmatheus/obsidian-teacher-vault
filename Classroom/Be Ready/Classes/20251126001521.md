The student practiced forming sentences like "The police accused Carl of stealing the car" and "Jamie insisted on paying for the meal." The instructor emphasized that these patterns don't follow logical rules and must be memorized. The lesson also covered the difference between "remind someone to do something" and "remind someone of something/someone," explaining that "remind of" is used when something resembles or brings to mind something else.

## Topic 3: Past Perfect Continuous Tense

The instructor introduced the past perfect continuous tense using "had been + verb-ing" to describe actions that happened before another past event. They practiced converting simple present sentences like "I am afraid" to past perfect continuous "I had already been afraid." The instructor explained that this tense is used to show that something started in the past and continued until another past event occurred. Examples included "I had already been afraid of the dark when she told me the story" and "I had been with her when the accident happened."

### Relevant Q&A

LUIZ HENRIQUE: I had already been afraid of the dark when she told me the story.

Be: Perfect. You placed this before the other past event. Which past event? "She told you the story." That's exactly what you need to do.

LUIZ HENRIQUE: I had been with her when the accident happened.

Be: Perfect. Sometimes in Portuguese we don't say "I had been" but in English, if you say "when the accident happened," you must use "I had been with her."
